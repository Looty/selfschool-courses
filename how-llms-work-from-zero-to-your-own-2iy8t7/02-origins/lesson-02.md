After this lesson you can say who started AI as a named field, when, and what bet it made.

## A summer proposal

On 2 September 1955 four researchers, John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester and Claude Shannon, formally submitted a proposal for a summer research project at Dartmouth College in Hanover, New Hampshire. The workshop took place in the summer of 1956, usually described as six weeks, though one participant's notes suggest closer to eight. [23]

## The bet

The proposal's central conjecture was that every aspect of learning, or any other feature of intelligence, can in principle be described so precisely that a machine can be made to simulate it. [23] This is the founding bet of the field: intelligence is something you can write down and run.

The proposal is also credited with introducing the term **artificial intelligence**. McCarthy chose it partly because it was neutral: it avoided narrowing the field to automata theory, and it steered clear of cybernetics, which centred on analog feedback. [23]

## What this means for the story ahead

Notice what the bet leaves open: *how* to describe intelligence precisely. Two broad answers competed for the next sixty years.

- **Write the rules down.** Experts encode knowledge as explicit rules, as ELIZA did in a simple form (lesson 8).
- **Let the machine learn the rules from examples.** The perceptron (lesson 10), backpropagation, and eventually language models.

Language models are the second answer winning, at enormous scale. Keeping both answers in mind explains why the field's history goes in cycles of optimism and disappointment.

:::key-takeaways
- The Dartmouth proposal was submitted on 2 September 1955; the workshop ran in summer 1956. [23]
- It conjectured that any feature of intelligence can in principle be described precisely enough for a machine to simulate it. [23]
- It is credited with introducing the term "artificial intelligence". [23]
:::

:::glossary
- **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: the field of making machines do things that seem to need intelligence; the term dates from the Dartmouth proposal.
- **Workshop**: a gathering of researchers for focused work over a period.
- **Conjecture**: a claim believed to be true but not yet proven.
- **Cybernetics**: an older field about control and feedback in machines and animals.
- **Automata theory**: the study of abstract machines and what they can compute.
- **Simulate**: reproduce the behaviour of something on a different system.
:::
